# Summary — what this package does

The **EU AML Package** is the biggest overhaul of EU anti-money-laundering law in a decade. It replaces the
patchwork of nationally-transposed AML directives with a directly-applicable **single rulebook** and a new
**EU-level supervisor**. Three instruments, all adopted 31 May 2024 and in force since mid-2024:

- **[AMLR](instruments/amlr-2024-1624.md)** — Regulation (EU) 2024/1624: the directly-applicable rulebook for
  obliged entities (customer due diligence, beneficial ownership, an expanded list of who is covered, the cash
  limit, reporting). **Applies from 10 July 2027.**
- **[AMLA](instruments/amla-2024-1620.md)** — Regulation (EU) 2024/1620: creates the **Authority for
  Anti-Money Laundering** in Frankfurt, which drafts the detailed rules (RTS/ITS/guidelines) and will directly
  supervise ~40 high-risk groups. **Operational since 1 July 2025.**
- **[AMLD6](instruments/amld6-2024-1640.md)** — Directive (EU) 2024/1640: the architecture layer (national
  supervisors, FIUs, beneficial-ownership registers) that Member States must **transpose by 10 July 2027**.

**Why it matters now.** The laws are settled; the live action is **implementation**. AMLA is running the
**Level-2/3 pipeline** — a fast cadence of consultations turning the AMLR/AMLD6 mandates into detailed binding
standards (the first wave is due to the Commission by **10 July 2026**). In parallel, Member States are
transposing AMLD6 — in the **Netherlands** via the **Iwt**, which replaces the Wwft entirely from 10 July 2027.

For a compliance audience, the headline operational shifts are: a directly-applicable rulebook (statutory
references move from Wwft/AMLD4 to the AMLR); more prescriptive CDD; a lower UBO threshold ("**25% or more**");
the **€3,000** Dutch cash limit (goods since 1 Jan 2026; services from 10 Jul 2027); new sectors in scope
(crypto-asset service providers, virtual IBANs, football, high-value goods); and EU-level direct supervision
from 2028.

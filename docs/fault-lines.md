# Fault lines — the contested issues

Where implementation choices, supervisors and stakeholders diverge most.

- **"Lastenluw" vs ATR4058 (NL).** The Dutch cabinet frames its implementation as "lastenluw" (minimal
  administrative burden); the regulatory-burden watchdog **ATR** found (advice ATR4058, 29 Sep 2025,
  "Implementatie Europees antiwitwaspakket niet lastenluw") that on several points it is **not**, making Dutch
  rules "more burdensome than necessary from an EU perspective." Record "lastenluw" as **intent**, not
  outcome. — [`netherlands.md`](netherlands.md) §d.

- **CDD redesign burden.** The [Art 28(1) CDD RTS](../extracts/amla/RTS-cdd-art28-1_consultation-paper.md#article-1) will
  fix detailed information/verification fields, likely forcing redesign of onboarding/KYC journeys. Industry
  (incl. the NVB) is pressing on proportionality and the interaction with existing DNB Q&As/Good Practices and
  NVB Risk-Based Industry Baselines, which AMLA's approach may diverge from. — [`stakeholders.md`](stakeholders.md).

- **UBO threshold reach.** "25% or more" widens the UBO population; an open question is whether the Commission
  sets a lower (15% or below) threshold for high-risk sectors. — [provision](provisions/ubo-threshold.md).

- **Scope expansion.** New obliged entities (CASPs, virtual IBANs, football, high-value goods) face
  building AML programmes from scratch ahead of 10 Jul 2027 (football: 2029). — [provision](provisions/scope-expansion-casps.md).

- **Divergence risk in the transition.** Legacy EBA guidelines remain valid until replaced (Art 54(5) AMLAR),
  so entities operate under old national/EBA guidance while the new AMLA instruments land — a moving
  compliance baseline.

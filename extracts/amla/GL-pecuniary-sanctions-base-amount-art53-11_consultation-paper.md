Draft Guidelines
on the base amounts for the imposition of pecuniary sanctions under Article 53(11) of Directive (EU) 2024/1640

## <a id="sec-1"></a>1. Subject matter, scope and definitions

### <a id="sec-1-1"></a>1.1 Subject matter and scope of application

These guidelines specify the base amounts for the imposition of pecuniary sanctions relative to turnover,
broken down per type of breach and category of obliged entities in accordance with Article 53(11) of
Directive (EU) 2024/1640.

### <a id="sec-1-2"></a>1.2 Addressees

These guidelines are addressed to supervisory authorities as defined in Article 2(1) point (46)[^1] and
supervisors as defined in Article 2(1), point (45)[^2] of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624. For the purpose of these
guidelines, the term "supervisors" refer to both "supervisory authorities" and "supervisors".

### <a id="sec-1-3"></a>1.3 Definitions

For the purpose of these guidelines, the terms and definitions set out in Regulation (EU) 2024/1624,
Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 and Directive (EU) 2024/1640 apply.

[^1]: Article 2(1)(46) AMLR): 'supervisory authority' means a supervisor who is a public body, or the public authority overseeing self-regulatory bodies in their performance of supervisory functions pursuant to Article 37 of Directive (EU) 2024/1640, or AMLA when acting as a supervisor.
[^2]: Article 2(1)(45) AMLR: 'supervisor' means the body entrusted with responsibilities aimed at ensuring compliance by obliged entities with the requirements of this Regulation, including AMLA when performing the tasks entrusted to it in Article 5(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1620.

## <a id="sec-2"></a>2. Implementation

### <a id="sec-2-1"></a>2.1 Date of application

These guidelines apply from 10 July 2027, except in relation to obliged entities referred to in Article 3,
points (3)(n) and (o), of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, to which it shall apply from 10 July 2029.

These guidelines shall not apply to proceedings related to pecuniary sanctions initiated before 10 July 2027.

## <a id="sec-3"></a>3. Guidelines

### <a id="sec-3-1"></a>3.1 General Considerations

1. When responding to a breach, supervisors may impose administrative measures, pecuniary sanctions or a
   combination of both.

2. In determining the appropriate response, supervisors should ensure that the action of the supervisor is
   effective proportionate to the breach and dissuasive.

3. When deciding on the amount of the pecuniary sanction to be imposed, supervisors should determine the base
   amount of the pecuniary sanction based on the type of the breach, the category of obliged entity, their
   legal form and by applying the relevant financial metric specified in these guidelines.

4. Breaches that are classified as category three or four in accordance with the Commission Delegated
   Regulation (EU) ………/…….[^3] ,are deemed to be serious, repeated or systematic in the meaning of
   Article 55(1) of Directive (EU), with the consequence that supervisors are obliged to impose pecuniary
   sanctions for the commitment of such breaches. The decision whether to impose pecuniary sanctions for
   breaches classified as category one or category two is left to the supervisory judgment of each supervisor.
   Where supervisors decide to impose a pecuniary sanction, they should determine the base amount in
   accordance with these Guidelines.

5. The base amount should serve as the starting point for determining the amount of the pecuniary sanction.
   After determining the base amount in accordance with these Guidelines, supervisors should consider
   additional factors[^4] to adjust the level of the pecuniary sanction. Accordingly, the final amount of the
   pecuniary sanction may be lower or higher than the base amount determined under these Guidelines.

[^3]: This will be updated upon the Commission Delegated Regulation under Article 53(10) of the AMLD being published in the OJEU.
[^4]: Article 53(6) of Directive (EU) 2024/1640 and the criteria under the Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) ………/……..

#### <a id="sec-3-1-1"></a>3.1.1 Proportionality

6. To respect the principle of proportionality, supervisors should determine the base amount of the pecuniary
   sanction by taking into account, at least:
   - I. the extent to which the breach exposes the internal market to ML/TF risk, with the base amount
     increasing progressively as the gravity of the breach increases; or
   - II. the extent to which the amount credibly enforces compliance objectives without exceeding what is
     necessary to achieve these objectives.

### <a id="sec-3-2"></a>3.2 Process to determine the base amount

7. Supervisors should follow a four-step approach to determine the base amount for the purposes of imposing a
   pecuniary sanction.

#### <a id="sec-3-2-1"></a>3.2.1 Step 1: Establish the type of breach

8. As a first step, supervisors should determine the type of breach in accordance with the classification set
   out in the Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) ………./………..[^5].

[^5]: This will be updated upon the Commission Delegated Regulation being published in the OJEU.

#### <a id="sec-3-2-2"></a>3.2.2 Step 2: Establish the category of obliged entity and the legal form

9. As a second step, supervisors should establish the category of obliged entity and the legal form.

**3.2.2.1 Category of Obliged Entity**

10. Supervisors should establish the category to which the obliged entity belongs in accordance with Article 3
    of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624. These Guidelines set out a range of base amounts expressed as a percentage of
    the relevant financial metric applicable to each category of obliged entities.

**3.2.2.2 Legal form**

11. Supervisors should distinguish between:
    - I. legal persons that are obliged entities;
    - II. natural persons that are:
      - (a) obliged entities;
      - (b) members of senior management of obliged entities and other natural persons who under national law
        are responsible for the breach of obligations stipulated by Regulations (EU) 2024/1624 and
        (EU) 2023/1113.

#### <a id="sec-3-2-3"></a>3.2.3 Step 3: Establish the relevant financial metric

12. As a third step, supervisors should:
    - I. establish the relevant financial metric applicable to the legal person or natural person;
    - II. decide on the independent and reliable source to obtain the relevant financial metric;

**3.2.3.1 Establish the relevant financial metric**

13. Supervisors should establish the relevant financial metric for determining the base amount of a pecuniary
    sanction by considering the following:
    - I. in case of a legal person supervisors should establish the relevant metric by determining the total
      annual turnover;
    - II. In case of a natural person, supervisors should establish the relevant metric by determining the
      total gross annual income.

14. Where total annual turnover or total gross annual income is not available or cannot be reliably determined,
    supervisors should use their supervisory judgement to identify an alternative relevant financial metric that
    provides the closest estimate of total annual turnover for legal persons or total gross annual income for
    natural persons.

**3.2.3.2 Independent and Reliable sources**

15. Supervisors should obtain the relevant financial metric from sources that are independent, reliable and
    complete. For this purpose, supervisors should be guided by the following hierarchy of sources:
    - I. audited financial statements;
    - II. tax declarations, tax assessment notices, or other equivalent official documentation issued by, filed
      with, or acknowledged by the competent tax authority;
    - III. regulatory reporting to competent authorities;
    - IV. management accounts;
    - V. official declarations; or
    - VI. reasoned estimation.

16. Recognising that the availability and reliability of sources may vary depending on the category of obliged
    entity and legal form, supervisors should determine which sources within the hierarchy are available and
    considered the most independent, reliable and complete for the purposes of determining the relevant
    financial metric.

17. Where supervisors have reasonable grounds to suspect that a source is not independent, reliable or complete,
    they should refer to other sources within the established hierarchy of sources. In doing so, supervisors
    should take a holistic view of all available information, including the reliability, consistency and
    completeness of the sources considered. They should assess whether, and to what extent, the additional
    information obtained affects the initial assessment, and apply any necessary adjustments accordingly.

18. Where supervisors have reasonable grounds to suspect that a source is not independent, reliable or complete,
    supervisors should have regard to the following considerations in determining an alternative source:
    - I. the credibility of the source, including its reputation;
    - II. the official status and independence of the information source;
    - III. the extent to which the information is up-to-date;
    - IV. the accuracy of the source, based on whether the information or data was subject to verification or
      validation or is consistent with other sources;
    - V. the ease with which the data provided can be forged.

**3.2.3.3 Period for determining the financial metric**

19. The relevant financial metric established under section 3.2.3.1 should be determined based on the latest
    available information. Where the use of the latest available information is not representative of the legal
    or natural person's financial circumstances, supervisors should consider, having regard to the period of
    the breach:
    - I. the financial metric corresponding to the period during which the breach occurred;
    - II. an average of the financial metric calculated over a period, taking into account the duration of the
      breach; or
    - III. a period that the supervisor considers proportionate and representative to the circumstances of the
      case.

#### <a id="sec-3-2-4"></a>3.2.4 Step 4: Set the base amount

20. After completing steps one to three, supervisors should determine the base amount of the pecuniary sanction
    by applying a percentage of the relevant financial metric established under section 3.2.3 of these
    guidelines.

21. Table 1 sets out the minimum and maximum percentages that supervisors should use to determine the base
    amount of a pecuniary sanction. When selecting the percentage within the applicable range, supervisors
    should ensure the base amount complies with section 3.1.1 of these guidelines and serves as an appropriate
    starting point for an effective, proportionate and dissuasive pecuniary sanction.

22. Supervisors should ensure that the base amount set respects the limits established under the applicable
    legal framework.

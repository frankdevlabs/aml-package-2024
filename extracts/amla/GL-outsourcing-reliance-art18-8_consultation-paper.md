### <a id="sec-1"></a>1 Compliance and reporting obligations

### <a id="sec-1-1"></a>1.1 Status of these Guidelines

1. This document contains guidelines issued pursuant to Articles 18(8) and 50 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624
and on AMLA's own initiative under Article 54 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1620.

2. These Guidelines set the AMLA view of appropriate practices of how Union law should be applied in a
particular area. They are addressed to supervisors as defined in Article 2(1) point (45), of Regulation (EU)
2024/1624 and obliged entities. All addressees must make every effort to comply with those guidelines by
incorporating them into their regulatory or supervisory framework in an appropriate manner.

### <a id="sec-1-2"></a>1.2 Reporting requirements

3. According to Article 54(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1620, each supervisor must notify AMLA as to whether
they (i) comply, (ii) do not comply, but intend to comply, or (iii) do not comply and do not intend to comply
with these Guidelines within two months from the issuance date.

4. In case of non-compliance, each supervisor must also notify AMLA within two months from the issuance date
of their reasons for not complying with these guidelines.

5. A template with instructions for notifications is available on AMLA's website.

6. Notifications should be filled in and submitted by persons with appropriate authority to report compliance
on behalf of their supervisor. Any change in the status of compliance must also be notified to AMLA.

7. Notifications will be published on the AMLA website, in line with Article 54(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1620.

### <a id="sec-2"></a>2 Subject matter, scope and definitions

### <a id="sec-2-1"></a>2.1 Subject matter, scope and addressees of the Guidelines

8. These Guidelines set out specific principles and rules obliged entities should apply when establishing
outsourcing pursuant to Article 18 of the AMLR, reliance on customer due diligence performed by other obliged
entities pursuant to Articles 48 and 49 of the AMLR, and address other third-party arrangements under the
anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing and non-implementation and evasion of targeted
financial sanctions (AML/CFT/TFS) framework as AMLA's own-initiative guidelines pursuant to Article 54(1) of
Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 (together, "the arrangements" for the purposes of these Guidelines). For the
avoidance of doubt, the reference to other third-party arrangements does not create a separate legal category
under the AMLR.

9. These Guidelines include three sets of guidelines:

   a) general Guidelines to be applied by obliged entities in all cases mentioned in paragraph 8;
   b) guidelines on outsourcing to be applied by obliged entities when establishing outsourcing arrangements
      pursuant to Article 18 of the AMLR. These Guidelines apply in addition to the General Guidelines;
   c) guidelines on reliance on customer due diligence by other obliged entities when establishing reliance
      arrangements pursuant to Articles 48 and 49 of the AMLR. These Guidelines apply in addition to the
      General Guidelines.

10. These Guidelines apply in conjunction with and without prejudice to any outsourcing or third-party risk
management requirements and/or expectations applicable under other Union frameworks. Obliged entities that
are subject to such requirements and/or expectations may make use of policies, procedures, controls,
documentation and contractual arrangements established under those frameworks, provided that the
AML/CFT/TFS-specific elements set out in these Guidelines are addressed with regard to the functions falling
within the scope of these Guidelines.

11. These Guidelines are addressed to obliged entities as defined in Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624
and supervisors as defined in Article 2(1) point (45) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 responsible for supervising
these obliged entities compliance with their AML/CFT/TFS obligations.

### <a id="sec-2-2"></a>2.2 Legislative references, abbreviations and definitions

LEGISLATIVE REFERENCES

- **AMLD** — Directive (EU) 2024/1640 of 31 May 2024 on the mechanisms to be put in place by Member States for
  the prevention of the use of the financial system for the purposes of money laundering or terrorist
  financing, amending Directive (EU) 2019/1937, and amending and repealing Directive (EU) 2015/849.
- **AMLR** — Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 of 31 May 2024 on the prevention of the use of the financial system for
  the purposes of money laundering or terrorist financing.
- **TFR** — Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 of 31 May 2023 on information accompanying transfers of funds and certain
  crypto-assets and amending Directive (EU) 2015/849.
- **AMLAR** — Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 of 31 May 2024 establishing the Authority for Anti-Money Laundering and
  Countering the Financing of Terrorism and amending Regulations (EU) No 1093/2010, (EU) No 1094/2010 and
  (EU) No 1095/2010.

ABBREVIATIONS: AML/CFT (anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism); AMLA (Authority for
Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism); KPIs (Key Performance Indicators); ML (money
laundering); TF (terrorist financing); TFS (targeted financial sanctions); CDD (Customer Due Diligence);
ML/TF (Money Laundering/Terrorist Financing).

DEFINITIONS

12. Unless otherwise specified, terms used and defined in the AMLR and AMLD have the same meaning in these
Guidelines. In addition, for the purposes of these Guidelines, the following definitions apply:

- **'Outsourcing'** means an arrangement of any form between an obliged entity and a service provider,
  including intra-group service providers, where the service provider performs one or more functions, or part
  thereof, falling within the scope of the AMLR or the TFR, that would otherwise be undertaken by the relevant
  obliged entity.
- **'Reliance on customer due diligence performed by other obliged entities'** means an arrangement between an
  obliged entity and another obliged entity or entities whether located in a Member State or a third country,
  where the former obliged entity pursuant to Articles 48 and 49 of the AMLR relies on the customer due
  diligence measures performed by the other obliged entity or entities for the purpose of complying with the
  requirements laid down in Article 20(1), points (a), (b) and (c) AMLR.
- **'Other third-party arrangement'** means an arrangement of any form other than outsourcing under Article 18
  AMLR or reliance under Articles 48 and 49 AMLR between an obliged entity and a service provider, including
  intra-group service providers, where the performance of the AMLR or TFR function is carried out by the
  obliged entity, supported by tools, instruments or services provided by a service provider.
- **'Subcontracting'** means an arrangement of any form where a service provider enters into an arrangement
  with another service provider, for the performance, in whole or in part, of a function under the AMLR or the
  TFR.
- **'Service provider'** means a natural or legal person providing or supporting a function by means of any
  type of arrangement.
- **'Intra-group service provider'** means a service provider that is part of a group or structure and that
  provides or supports functions to obliged entity(es) within the same group or structure.
- **'Function'** means any process, service or activity or part of it under the scope of the AMLR or TFR.
- **'Critical function'** means a function, the defect or failure of which would materially impair the
  continuing compliance of the obliged entity with the requirements set out in the AMLR or TFR.

> ▸ Footnote in source: intra-group service providers include, for the purpose of these Guidelines, service
> providers within the same structure pursuant to Article 16(4) AMLR; subsequent references to "group" should
> be understood as including structures having common ownership, management, compliance controls or a
> combination of them pursuant to Article 16(4) AMLR.

### <a id="sec-4"></a>4 Guidelines

### <a id="sec-4-1"></a>4.1 General Guidelines

16. Obliged entities may make use of the arrangements to support compliance with their AMLR and TFR
obligations in line with the General Guidelines set out in this Section. Such arrangements can enhance the
effectiveness, efficiency, consistency and scalability of their compliance with AMLR and TFR obligations where
they are appropriately designed, implemented and overseen.

17. Obliged entities should ensure that their arrangements are designed and implemented in a manner
proportionate to the nature, risks and complexity of their business, as well as the size of the obliged
entity. In applying the principle of proportionality, obliged entities should take into account their specific
circumstances, including the overall ML/TF risk profile, business model, size and the nature, scale and
complexity of their activities and operations, including for the use of tools, methods and processes used to
manage and control such arrangements.

18. The use of the arrangements should not transfer the responsibility of the obliged entity for compliance
with AMLR or TFR obligations under any circumstances. The obliged entity should ensure that it retains
sufficient control, effective understanding of risks and management of its AML/CFT/TFS tasks also when making
use of the arrangements. The responsibility for compliance with AMLR and TFR requirements should rest
ultimately with the management body or equivalent of the obliged entity without prejudice to national
provisions on joint civil or criminal liability of management bodies.

19. Subject to proportionality considerations related to size, obliged entities should establish a dedicated
role for monitoring the arrangements or designate a member of the management body or equivalent, who is
directly accountable for overseeing the arrangements.

20. Obliged entities should have in place a framework on the sound management of risks arising from the
arrangements used for AML/CFT/TFS purposes. The identified risks should be reflected in the business-wide risk
assessment referred to in Article 10 AMLR and should apply, where relevant, at group and entity level as
appropriate. Obliged entities should maintain appropriate understanding of risks, especially when they have in
place arrangements covering substantial parts of AML/CFT tasks.

21. Where the obliged entity forms part of a group, such framework should be established at the level of the
parent undertaking in the Union. The parent undertaking should ensure good governance on the development and
consistent implementation of the arrangements within the group. Where the obliged entity is not part of a
group, such framework should be established and maintained at obliged entity level.

22. When undertaking any arrangement, obliged entities should retain sufficient resources to the extent
possible, expertise, risk understanding, capacity and governance arrangements to effectively control the
functions performed under the arrangements proportionate to the nature, scale and complexity of the
arrangement, entity characteristics, and the associated AML/CFT/TFS risks. At the same time, arrangements
should not be structured in a way that transfers, obscures or dilutes the obliged entities' responsibility,
risk assessment and effective control over AML/CFT requirements or circumvents AML/CFT requirements. To this
end, obliged entities should in particular:

   a) maintain a clear and transparent organisational framework for the management of the arrangements and
      assign clear responsibilities for the establishment, management and monitoring of the arrangements;
   b) allocate sufficient resources, expertise and capacity to understand, assess and control the functions
      concerned and the associated risks to the extent possible;
   c) ensure that adequate policies, procedures and controls are in place to review the effectiveness of such
      arrangements and manage the associated AML/CFT/TFS risks;
   d) ensure that adequate information on the arrangements and the AML/CFT/TFS risk assessment are provided to
      the management body or equivalent on a regular basis.

23. Obliged entities should have in place written policies and procedures on outsourcing and reliance on
customer due diligence performed by other obliged entities. For other third-party arrangements obliged
entities should have in place written policies and procedures and determine their level of details on a
risk-based approach. The level of detail of such policies and procedures should be proportionate to the size,
nature and complexity of the obliged entity, to the nature and risks of the arrangements concerned and their
potential impact on compliance with AML/CFT and TFR obligations.

24. The management body or equivalent should review the written policies annually or, where justified on the
basis of proportionality, at other less frequent regular intervals. In any event, obliged entities should
ensure that policies remain up to date and appropriate to their risk profile. Changes made to the policies
should be implemented in a timely and efficient manner.

25. Risk-based and proportionality considerations may lead obliged entities to make use of policies,
procedures and controls developed and implemented under other Union sectoral frameworks such as prudential,
conduct or information technology frameworks while still respecting the AMLR, the TFR and these Guidelines. In
addition, policies and procedures governing reliance on customer due diligence performed by other obliged
entities or other third-party arrangements may be merged with the policies and procedures on outsourcing under
these Guidelines. However, obliged entities should ensure that such policies and procedures clearly
distinguish between, and set out the specific requirements applicable to, outsourcing, reliance on customer
due diligence performed by other obliged entities, and other third-party arrangements as appropriate.

26. Obliged entities should ensure that individual arrangements are appropriately identified, documented,
assessed and monitored, taking into account at least the nature, complexity and risks of the arrangement, the
nature of the functions concerned and their potential impact on compliance with AML and TFR obligations.

27. Before entering an individual arrangement, obliged entities should determine whether the arrangement
constitutes:

   a) outsourcing;
   b) reliance on customer due diligence performed by other obliged entities;
   c) any other third-party arrangement such as the use or acquisition of third-party software or the access
      to databases or screening services.

28. Obliged entities entering arrangements referred to in paragraph 27 (c) should carry out themselves the
relevant AML/CFT/TFS function, unless that function is subject to separate outsourcing or reliance on customer
due diligence arrangement(s). The use of tools, systems, data sources or supporting services that do not
involve the service provider carrying out the AML/CFT/TFS function on behalf of the obliged entity would not
constitute outsourcing. Safeguards for other third-party arrangements should be proportionate to the nature
and risks of such arrangements as well as to the extent to which the other third-party arrangement materially
influences the performance of the AML/CFT/TFS function.

29. When making use of an individual arrangement, obliged entities should ensure that the prospective service
provider is suitable to carry out such arrangement by:

   a) performing a risk assessment;
   b) conducting due diligence.

30. Individual arrangements should be governed by a written agreement or, where allowed under the AMLR, by an
internal procedure established at group level. Such documents should be clear, comprehensive, enforceable,
complete and should appropriately reflect the allocation of AML/CFT/TFS responsibilities between the obliged
entity and the relevant service provider or obliged entity.

31. The rights and obligations of the parties should be documented in a record, such as a register, database
or equivalent as appropriate and be readily available in written form upon request of supervisors, competent
authorities, external auditors when applicable or other appointed third parties. Such record should include at
least the name and location of the service provider or obliged entity, the type of arrangement, and the
AML/CFT tasks covered by the relevant arrangement. Risk-based and proportionality considerations may lead
obliged entities to make use of records such as register, database or equivalent developed and implemented
under other Union sectoral frameworks such as prudential, conduct or information technology frameworks while
still respecting the AMLR, the TFR and these Guidelines.

32. The written agreement or, where allowed under the AMLR, the internal procedure established at group level
applicable to the individual arrangement should include at least the following elements as appropriate:

   a) key elements of the arrangement: i) the AML/CFT/TFS tasks subject to the arrangement and their scope of
      application; ii) responsibilities of the obliged entity and the service provider or other obliged
      entity; iii) locations including service provision and data storage as applicable; iv) reporting and
      escalation arrangements in particular to the obliged entity; v) performance expectations; vi) AMLR and
      TFR compliance.
   b) provisions on accountability and control: i) a statement saying that the obliged entity remains
      responsible for AMLR and TFR compliance as well as for decision-making and judgement on the arrangement;
      ii) a statement saying that service providers should act only within the agreed mandate and should fully
      cooperate with the obliged entity, supervisors, competent authorities, external auditors when applicable
      and other appointed third parties.
   c) provisions on governance and risk management: i) measures ensuring that the arrangement does not impair
      the obliged entity's ability to monitor, control, and manage associated risks; ii) information sharing;
      iii) incident management; iv) identification, assessment, mitigation and management of potential conflict
      of interest; v) data protection; vi) subcontracting conditions especially to address supply chain risks
      where applicable.
   d) provisions on safeguarding robust controls, audit, access and exit rights: i) granting access to
      relevant information, inspection and audit rights to the obliged entity and supervisors in compliance
      with Union and national laws; ii) reporting obligations to the obliged entity; iii) the obligation to
      inform the obliged entities of AML/CFT/TFS related deficiencies detected by the relevant supervisors,
      external or internal audit and affecting the services provided, as well as the corrective measures
      undertaken to address such deficiencies; iv) transition and exit arrangements that ensure continuity of
      AMLR and TFR compliance.

33. Without prejudice to the requirements set out under Regulation (EU) 2016/679, obliged entities, when
setting up arrangements with service providers or obliged entities located in particular in third countries,
should take into account differences in national provisions regarding the protection of data. Obliged entities
should ensure that the individual arrangement includes the obligation that the service provider protects
confidential, personal or otherwise sensitive information and complies with all legal requirements regarding
the protection of data that apply to the obliged entity, in particular when the service provider or the
obliged entity is located in third countries and the obliged entity provides them with personal data.

34. Obliged entities should ensure that the arrangements involving service providers established or located in
third countries are subject to appropriate additional safeguards so that they do not increase the risk of
non-compliance with AMLR and TFR or impair effective supervision. Obliged entities should be able to
demonstrate such additional safeguards to supervisors upon request.

35. Obliged entities should ensure that the use of technological solutions, including third-party software,
databases or services for AML/CFT/TFS purposes that make use of automated processes or involving AI systems,
does not prevent the obliged entity, its internal control functions, external auditors when applicable,
supervisors or competent authorities from understanding, reviewing or assessing the manner in which such
solutions support compliance with applicable AMLR and TFR requirements.

36. The arrangements should be subject to ongoing controls, monitoring and periodic reviews, the frequency,
scope and intensity of which should be determined on a risk-based and proportionate basis, taking into account
the nature, scale and complexity of the arrangements and the associated AML/CFT/TFS risks as well as the
nature of the functions concerned and their potential impact on the compliance with the AMLR and the TFR
obligations. Obliged entities should periodically update risk assessments and report the risks identified in
respect of such arrangements to the management body or equivalent on a risk-based approach.

37. Without prejudice to the provisions set out in paragraph 32(d), arrangements should ensure that the
exercise of the audit, access and supervisory rights of the obliged entity, supervisors and the competent
authorities is not restricted, including where the service provider is established in a third country, and that
such arrangements are compatible with the supervisory powers conferred under applicable Union and national
law.

38. Without prejudice to their ultimate responsibility for compliance with AML/CFT obligations, their
obligation to effectively monitor and control outsourced activities, and individual audit rights, obliged
entities may use:

   a) pooled audits organised jointly with other clients of the same service providers and performed by them
      and these clients or by a third party appointed by them; and
   b) relevant third-party certifications and third-party or internal audit reports, made available by the
      service provider.

39. Obliged entities should, on a risk-based and proportionate basis, have in place, maintain and, where
relevant, periodically test appropriate business continuity and contingency plans, audit plans and programmes,
and transition arrangements, taking into account the nature, complexity and risks of the function concerned.

40. Obliged entities should ensure that they are able to terminate or exit the arrangements without undue
disruption to their compliance with AML/CFT/TFS requirements.

### <a id="guideline-1"></a>4.2 Guideline 1: Outsourcing

CLASSIFICATION OF CRITICAL FUNCTIONS

41. Obliged entities should assess whether an outsourced function is critical on a case-by-case basis, and
consider function as critical where the defect or failure of the function would materially impair the
continuing compliance of the obliged entity with the requirements set out in the AMLR or TFR. Subject to any
relevant assessment, the following functions should be in principle considered critical:

   a) customer due diligence obligations, including remote customer due diligence;
   b) transaction monitoring obligations;
   c) obligations to comply with TFS;
   d) reporting requirements under Article 69 AMLR when allowed under Article 18 AMLR;
   e) internal controls.

42. In making the assessment referred to in the previous paragraph, obliged entities should consider at least:

   a) the potential impact of a disruption or failure on the obliged entity's ability to comply with its
      AML/CFT obligations;
   b) the ability to effectively identify, assess, monitor and mitigate ML/TF/TFS risks;
   c) the concentration, aggregation and dependency risks arising from the use of the service provider;
   d) the accessibility to relevant data;
   e) the ability and time needed to bring the outsourced function back in-house or transfer it to another
      service provider.

43. The assessment referred to in the previous paragraph should be documented in a manner that is transparent,
in particular to the independent audit functions when present, external auditors when applicable, the
supervisor and the competent authorities. Obliged entities should be able to demonstrate such assessment to
the supervisors upon request.

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

44. In addition to the expectations provided in the General Guidelines as set out in paragraph 31, obliged
entities should maintain an updated record, such as a register, database or equivalent, of outsourcing
arrangements at individual and at group level that contains at least the following:

   a) general information about the service provider, including corporate registration number and/or legal
      identity identifier (where available) and its location;
   b) description of the outsourced tasks, including the rationale for outsourcing the specific tasks, and the
      planned starting date of the outsourcing arrangement and end date if available;
   c) documented assessment of the service provider's compliance to provide the outsourced tasks;
   d) whether or not the service provider is part of the same group or structure;
   e) any relevant information related to the criticality of the function as deemed appropriate;
   f) in case the function is considered non-critical, reasoning for the non-criticality assessment;
   g) in case of subcontractors, information on the subcontractors used for critical functions as applicable.

WRITTEN AGREEMENTS

45. In addition to the expectations set out in paragraph 32 of the General Guidelines, the written agreement
establishing the outsourcing arrangement should include the following elements:

   a) provisions stating that the service provider(s) act on behalf of the obliged entity solely for the
      execution of the agreed tasks and do not assume other AML/CFT/TFS compliance, governance or
      decision-making responsibilities other than those specified in the written agreement;
   b) provisions on availability, authenticity, integrity, and confidentiality in relation to the protection
      of data, including personal data;
   c) provisions ensuring that the data that are owned by the obliged entity can be easily accessed, recovered,
      and returned in the case of the insolvency, resolution, or discontinuation of business operations of the
      service provider or in the event of the termination of the contractual arrangements.

46. In addition to the elements indicated in the previous paragraph, obliged entities should include the
following elements in the written agreement establishing the outsourcing of critical functions:

   a) KPIs or equivalent that allow the obliged entity to measure the effectiveness of the outsourcing
      arrangement and take immediate actions, if the provision of functions is not met;
   b) notice periods and explicit reporting obligations of the service provider to the obliged entity,
      especially on any development that may have a material impact on the service provider's ability to
      provide the outsourced services;
   c) the rights for the obliged entity to monitor the performance of the service provider on an ongoing basis,
      in particular providing for: i) unrestricted rights of access of obliged entities and supervisors to
      inspect and audit the service provider; ii) obligation of the service provider to fully cooperate with
      the obliged entity, supervisors and any other appointed third-party during onsite inspections and audits;
      iii) obligation of the service provider to provide any information to address requests from inspections or
      audits immediately and in any case as soon as practicable;
   d) the obligation to notify immediately and in any case as soon as practicable the obliged entity of any
      material disruption, defect or failure affecting the provision of the function;
   e) exit strategies establishing a mandatory adequate transition period allowing: i) the continuation of
      outsourced functions; and ii) migration to another service provider or change to in-house solutions
      consistent with the complexity of the outsourced function.

RISK ASSESSMENT

47. As part of the risk assessment mentioned in paragraph 29, before establishing individual outsourcing
arrangements, obliged entities should have in place a risk assessment that should consider:

   a) the size and complexity of the outsourced functions;
   b) the scope and extent of the outsourced functions;
   c) the potential impact of the outsourcing arrangement on the ability of the obliged entity to: i) identify,
      monitor and manage relevant AML/CFT/TFS risks; ii) conduct appropriate audits regarding the function
      provided by the service provider;
   d) the potential impact on the services provided to their clients;
   e) the regulated status of the service provider under the AMLR or equivalent in third countries;
   f) the use of a service provider within the same group or structure;
   g) the potential impact of the outsourcing arrangement on the organisation and effectiveness of internal
      controls and on the compliance with the AMLR and TFR;
   h) the criticality assessment of the outsourced functions;
   i) the protection of data and the potential impact of confidentiality breach or failure in line with
      applicable regulation;
   j) the availability and feasibility of alternative solutions, such as reintegration of the task or
      replacement of the service provider, in case of termination, taking into account the overall AML/CFT/TFS
      risks of the arrangement.

48. Obliged entities should assess the following additional factors as elements when conducting the risk
assessment of critical functions:

   a) the concentration and aggregation risks at obliged entity level of AML/CFT/TFS tasks;
   b) the risks arising from the use of subcontracting arrangements for the same task;
   c) the risks arising from operating a fragmented AML/CFT/TFS framework by multiple service providers to
      perform several AML/CFT/TFS functions across the obliged entity;
   d) the potential difficulties in monitoring compliance;
   e) the involvement of the compliance function as appropriate;
   f) third-country risks from the use of service providers established in third countries as applicable.

49. Proportionality considerations may lead to a simplified risk assessment when obliged entities intend to
outsource functions to other obliged entities or service providers within the same group and established or
located in the Union, in particular for factors related to the intra-group service provider, while still
retaining sufficient controls on the outsourcing arrangement.

DUE DILIGENCE

50. As part of the due diligence mentioned in paragraph 29 and subject to proportionality considerations,
before establishing an individual outsourcing arrangement, factors to be considered when conducting the due
diligence on a potential service provider should include:

   a) its business model, nature, ownership, group and organisation;
   b) operational and technical capability and track record, including the ability to run long-term
      relationships based on prior engagements with obliged entities or other entities;
   c) whether the service provider is the parent undertaking in the Union;
   d) whether the service provider is subject to AML/CFT/TFS supervision;
   e) whether the service provider established or located in third countries has adequate knowledge and
      expertise in Union AML/CFT/TFS legislation.

51. In addition to the elements indicated in the previous paragraph, in the case of outsourcing of critical
functions, the obliged entity should ensure that the service provider has:

   a) business reputation, appropriate and sufficient capacity to comply with AML/CFT/TFS requirements, and
      human and technical resources to deliver the relevant tasks;
   b) where appropriate, an AML/CFT compliance function, having regard to the nature and risks of the
      outsourced function;
   c) appropriate internal controls and risk management processes and procedures, especially in cases when
      subcontracting arrangements are envisaged;
   d) appropriate tools and systems to ensure compliance with the AMLR and TFR requirements at all times;
   e) knowledge of the geographic dependencies and management of the related risks, especially if the service
      provider is established or located in a third country or is not part of the same group;
   f) business continuity plans, contingency plans and other relevant plans;
   g) proper technical arrangements to ensure that audits, including onsite inspections, can be effectively
      performed by the obliged entity itself, appointed third parties, supervisors and competent authorities.

SUBCONTRACTING OF OUTSOURCED CRITICAL FUNCTIONS

52. In addition to the expectations provided in the General Guidelines as set out in paragraph 32, outsourcing
arrangements of critical functions should specify whether subcontracting of functions, or material parts of
it, is permitted, and in that case what conditions are applicable to such subcontracting.

53. The written agreement between the obliged entity and the service provider of critical functions should:

   a) put in place safeguards ensuring that the outsourcing arrangement remains effective throughout the
      subcontracting chain, including with regard to service continuity, access to information and access,
      information and audit rights by obliged entities, supervisors, competent authorities and appointed third
      parties;
   b) specify that the service provider should notify the obliged entity about any new subcontracting
      arrangements and any intended changes to its subcontracting arrangement. The notification period should
      allow the obliged entity at least to carry out a risk assessment of the proposed changes and to object
      before the planned subcontracting or material changes come into effect. Obliged entities should have a
      right to object to the intended subcontracting or material changes thereof, or for explicit approval to
      be required.

54. Where outsourcing arrangements include the possibility that the service provider subcontracts critical
functions to subcontractors, obliged entities should take into account:

   a) the additional risks that may arise if the subcontractor is established or located in a third country or
      a different country from the service provider; and
   b) the risk that long and complex chains of subcontracting reduce the ability of obliged entities to oversee
      the critical function and the ability of supervisors to effectively supervise them.

55. Obliged entities should specify in their written agreements monitoring and reporting obligations of
subcontractors towards service providers, and, where agreed, towards the obliged entity in line with the
contractual obligations between the service provider and the obliged entity.

56. Obliged entities should ensure that service providers identify all subcontractors and oversee them on a
regular basis.

ACCESS, INFORMATION AND AUDIT RIGHTS

57. In addition to the expectations provided in the General Guidelines, outsourcing arrangements should grant
obliged entities, appointed third parties and supervisors the following:

   a) access to the relevant business premises as well as the right to take copies of relevant information and
      documentation;
   b) unrestricted rights of inspection and auditing related to outsourcing arrangements to enable them to
      monitor the arrangement and to ensure compliance with all applicable regulatory and contractual
      requirements.

58. In addition to the expectations provided in the General Guidelines, in cases of outsourcing of critical
functions, obliged entities should assess:

   a) when relying on pooled audits as referred to in paragraph 38(a), whether they have sufficient
      information and are sufficiently involved in scoping, planning, performing and reporting the audit and
      its findings. Obliged entities should make an appropriate assessment of the scope and content of such
      pooled audits and may still decide to perform individual audits at their discretion;
   b) whether the third-party certifications and third-party or internal audit reports as referred to in
      paragraph 38(b) are adequate and sufficient to comply with their AMLR and TFR obligations. Obliged
      entities should not rely solely on these certifications and reports over time.

TERMINATION RIGHTS

59. In addition to the expectations provided in the General Guidelines as set out in paragraph 40, the
outsourcing arrangement of critical functions should ensure that there are appropriate mechanisms to terminate
and transfer the function by the service provider to another service provider or to reintegrate the functions
to the obliged entity. The outsourcing arrangement of critical function should at least:

   a) clearly set out the obligations of the existing service provider in the case of transfer of function,
      including the treatment of data;
   b) set an appropriate transition period, during which the third-party service provider, after the
      termination of the arrangement, would continue to provide the function performed to reduce risks of
      disruptions;
   c) include an obligation of the third-party service provider to support the obliged entity in the orderly
      transfer of the function in the event of the termination of the outsourcing arrangement;
   d) require the service provider, upon termination of the arrangement, to actively facilitate the orderly
      return or transfer of the obliged entity's data and, thereafter, to securely delete any remaining data
      within an appropriate timeframe, unless its continued retention is required by applicable law.

SUPERVISORY EXPECTATIONS ON PRIOR NOTIFICATIONS OF OUTSOURCING ARRANGEMENTS UNDER ARTICLE 18(1) AMLR

60. According to Article 18(1) AMLR, an obliged entity shall notify the supervisor of the outsourcing before
the service provider starts to carry out the outsourced task. For that purpose, the notification should
include, as a minimum, the content indicated in Annex I.

61. As a general rule, the notification should be submitted in writing in a timely manner. Obliged entities
should aim to submit the notification within 28 working days in advance of the planned start of the outsourced
functions, except in justified cases where this is not possible.

62. To ensure simplification and reduce administrative burden, before the service provider(s) starts to carry
out the outsourced task in line with the previous paragraph,

   a) obliged entities may decide to submit one outsourcing notification covering more than one or multiple
      service providers under a framework written outsourcing agreement to the appropriate supervisor so long
      as all the relevant information indicated in Annex I is included in the notification;
   b) the parent undertaking or any coordinating obliged entity may submit notifications on behalf of more
      obliged entities within the same group to the appropriate supervisors, so long as all the relevant
      information indicated in Annex I is included in the notification.

63. Should any material elements and/or severe events impact the outsourcing arrangement after the initial
notification, this amounts to a new outsourcing arrangement and a new notification shall be submitted. Material
elements and/or severe events include changes to:

   a) the service provider(s);
   b) scope of outsourced AML/CFT/TFS task(s);
   c) location of the service provider, functions or data when such change materially impacts the outsourcing
      arrangement;
   d) subcontracting chains.

APPLICATIONS FOR PRIOR APPROVAL OF OUTSOURCING ARRANGEMENTS UNDER ARTICLE 18(7) AMLR

64. According to Article 18(7) AMLR, collective investment undertakings where a collective investment
undertaking has no legal personality, or has only a board of directors and has delegated the processing of
subscriptions and the collection of funds as defined in Article 4, point (25), of Directive (EU) 2015/2366
from investors to another entity may outsource the tasks indicated under Article 18(3) points c), d) and e)
AMLR in particular situations, provided that it has notified its intention to outsource to the supervisor and
the supervisor has approved such outsourcing arrangement. For that purpose, the notification should include,
as a minimum, the content indicated in Annex II.

65. The assessment, including any further request for information, should take account of the service
provider's existing regulatory status, the obliged entity's regulatory status, the relevant authorisations
and supervisory framework, where applicable.

### <a id="guideline-2"></a>4.3 Guideline 2: Reliance on customer due diligence performed by other obliged entities

GENERAL REQUIREMENTS FOR RELIANCE ARRANGEMENTS

66. Where permitted, obliged entities may rely on the customer due diligence performed by other obliged
entities for the purposes of the initial customer identification requirements laid down in Article 20(1),
points (a), (b), and (c) of the AMLR. Where appropriate and in line with the AMLR and these Guidelines, such
reliance can be considered to avoid unnecessary duplication of customer due diligence measures, in particular
where two or more obliged entities are required to perform customer due diligence on the same customer,
including where those obliged entities belong to the same group.

67. Obliged entities should ensure that reliance arrangements are structured in such a way that the obliged
entity relied upon performs its role and is responsible itself for complying with the reliance arrangement.

68. Obliged entities that enter into reliance arrangements pursuant to Articles 48 and 49 AMLR should ensure
that such arrangements are used exclusively to facilitate access to initial customer due diligence information
to fulfil the measures laid down in Article 20(1), points (a), (b) and (c) of AMLR.

69. In cases of reliance on an obliged entity within the same group, the relying obliged entity can calibrate
and simplify the reliance framework with such obliged entities in line with a risk-based approach. In any case,
pursuant to Article 48(3) AMLR, the relying obliged entity shall ensure that:

   a) there are no obstacles to the application of the group-wide requirements by the obliged entity relied
      upon;
   b) information sharing with the obliged entity relied upon is subject to sufficient guarantees in terms of
      confidentiality, data protection and the use of information as well as fully respecting fundamental
      rights.

RISK ASSESSMENT AND DUE DILIGENCE

70. As part of the risk assessment and due diligence mentioned in paragraph 29, prior to entering into a
reliance arrangement, the relying obliged entity should perform a documented pre-reliance risk and due
diligence assessment of the obliged entity relied upon and in particular whether the obliged entity relied
upon:

   a) meets the requirements of Article 48(1) of AMLR;
   b) is of good repute and has in place adequate AML/CFT policies, procedures and controls, especially in the
      case of an obliged entity established in a third country;
   c) identifies, assesses, mitigate and manage any potential conflict of interest;
   d) informs the relying obliged entities as soon as practicable when the affected customer relationship(s)
      are terminated.

71. As regards the CDD information made available to the relying obliged entity, obliged entities should
ensure that such information:

   a) is complete, accurate and up to date;
   b) is consistent with the requirements of Articles 20, 22 and 25 of AMLR;
   c) is sufficiently detailed to allow the relying obliged entity to fulfil its own obligations;
   d) allows the relying obliged entity to easily verify compliance with the reliance arrangement and
      applicable AMLR requirements, including by auditing such information.

72. The relying obliged entity should ensure that the obliged entity relied upon complies with Article 22(6)
AMLR provisions, and in addition confirms that:

   a) the relying obliged entity can assess and review the procedure in place by the obliged entity relied upon
      for remote customer due diligence, and should be able to validate it for such purposes prior to relying
      on it;
   b) the relying obliged entity can obtain the customer identification information and supporting documentation
      promptly upon request and, in any event, within five working days, also in cases of remote identification
      processes;
   c) reliance is complemented by additional measures where the level of risk so requires.

73. In case the relying obliged entity has doubts or concerns on the information collected from the obliged
entity relied upon, the relying obliged entity should take appropriate measures, including carrying out itself
the customer due diligence measures applicable for the relevant customer(s) and consider whether enhanced
oversight of the arrangement is necessary.

74. When justified and on a risk-based approach, obliged entities may:

   a) limit the pre-reliance risk and due diligence assessments provided in paragraph 70 to verifying the
      conditions laid down in Article 48(1) AMLR in cases where the prospected obliged entity relied upon: i) is
      part of the same group; ii) is established or located in the Union; iii) has in place adequate AML/CFT
      policies, procedures and controls.
   b) use standardised reliance frameworks or industry-wide template arrangements that are appropriate to put
      in place specific arrangements, insofar as they are compliant with AML/CFT/TFS requirements.

WRITTEN AGREEMENTS OR INTERNAL PROCEDURES

75. In addition to the elements mentioned in the General Guidelines as set out in paragraph 32, the written
agreement or the internal procedures in case of intra-group reliance should include at least the following
elements:

   a) provisions stating that reliance on other obliged entity is put in place only to meet the requirements
      laid down in Article 20(1), points (a), (b) and (c) AMLR;
   b) obligations of the obliged entity relied upon to provide the relying obliged entity, without delay and in
      any event within 5 working days, with copies and information concerning the identification and
      verification of the customer, the customer's beneficial owners or persons on whose behalf the customer
      acts, as well as information on the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship;
   c) provisions stating where and how the relevant data is collected, managed and/or stored;
   d) obligations to receive a copy of the documentation collected by the obliged entity relied upon or the
      original documentation when the obliged entity relied upon terminates its business relationship with the
      relevant customer, or when it is not an obliged entity anymore, subject to data protection compliance.

## <a id="annex-i"></a>ANNEX I — List of information to be included in notifications of outsourcing arrangements under Article 18(1) AMLR

```text
Obliged entities should include at least the following information in the notifications under Article 18(1)
of the AMLR:

  a) general information about the obliged entity;
  b) general information about the service provider, such as an identifier (LEI, EUID for legal persons or
     other alternative identifiers), the corporate registration number, the registered address and other
     relevant contact details, and the name of its ultimate parent undertaking and an identifier (if any);
  c) description of the outsourced task(s), including general information on the reasons for outsourcing the
     specific tasks;
  d) where applicable, the identity and location of the subcontractor(s);
  e) the location where the outsourced tasks will be conducted;
  f) the planned starting date and duration of the outsourcing arrangement;
  g) other information relevant to the outsourcing arrangement such as whether the outsourcing is of critical
     function(s) and whether the outsourcing is systematic.

Additional information that obliged entities should consider submitting in cases of outsourcing of critical
functions:

  a) information on the risk assessment of the outsourcing arrangement, as well as on the due diligence
     process and the outcomes of the processes;
  b) information on the business continuity and contingency plan;
  c) information on the internal governance of the outsourcing arrangement, including the organisation for
     monitoring performance of outsourced tasks by the service provider against quality standards.

Additional information that obliged entities should submit in cases of service providers located or
established in third countries identified pursuant to Section 2 of Chapter III AMLR:

  a) confirmation that the service provider belongs to the same group as the obliged entity;
  b) confirmation that the group applies AML/CFT/TFS policies and procedures, customer due diligence measures
     and rules on record-keeping applicable to the group that are fully in compliance with the AMLR or
     equivalent rules in third countries;
  c) confirmation that the requirements referred to in point b) are effectively implemented at group level by
     the supervisory authority of the home Member State in accordance with Chapter IV of AMLD.
```
